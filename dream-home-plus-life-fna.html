Before we start

Why does this "need analysis" even matter?

Most people buy insurance or investments the way they buy a lottery ticket — a little hope, no real plan. A real need analysis flips that around.

  • 1 We look at what your family would actually lose — income, time, stability — if life threw a curveball tomorrow.
  • 2 We look at what you're quietly building — savings, retirement, your kids' future — and whether it's actually on track.
  • 3 The money you set aside for retirement isn't just a number — done right, it takes care of you the way you once took care of everyone else.
  • 4 And if there's no estate plan, a big chunk of what you built can quietly go to taxes — leaving your kids to sell what you worked for, or borrow against it, just to pay the bill.
Before we start

One more thing most people never get told.

Even the RRSPs and mutual funds sitting quietly in your bank can still get tied up in probate when you pass away — meaning delays, legal fees, and public court records before your family sees a dollar.

Segregated funds are similar investment funds, but with one key difference — they let you name a direct beneficiary. That one feature alone lets them bypass probate entirely, passing directly to that person, faster and more privately.

So the real question isn't just "do I have savings" — it's "will my family actually get this quickly, and in full, when it matters most?"

Families with no protection plan don't fail because they didn't care. They fail because no one ever showed them the real number.

Education plans quietly compound too — just like debt does.

A small monthly amount started early for a child's education grows enormously over 15-18 years thanks to compounding and government grants. Waiting even 5 years to start can cut the end result nearly in half.
